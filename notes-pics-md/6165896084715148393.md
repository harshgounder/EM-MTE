# Transformer Tests: Open Circuit and Short Circuit

The open-circuit test is also called the **no-load test**. Transformer efficiency can be determined from two standard tests:

1. Open-circuit test.
2. Short-circuit test.

## Open-circuit test

Measured no-load quantities are \(V_0\), \(I_0\), and \(W_0\).

\[
\cos\theta_0=\frac{W_0}{V_0I_0}
\]

\[
I_w=I_0\cos\theta_0, \qquad I_m=I_0\sin\theta_0
\]

\[
\sin\theta_0=\sqrt{1-\cos^2\theta_0}
\]

The shunt-branch parameters, referred to the test side, are:

\[
R_0=\frac{V_0}{I_w}, \qquad X_0=\frac{V_0}{I_m}
\]

Rated voltage is applied, usually to the low-voltage winding, while the other winding is open. As the no-load current is small, typically 1% to 3% of rated current, copper loss \(I_0^2R\) is neglected. Thus \(W_0\) is approximately the core (iron) loss.

## Short-circuit test

One winding is short-circuited and a low adjustable voltage is applied to the other, commonly the high-voltage side. The voltage is increased only until rated current flows.

- The test determines the series, or winding, parameters.
- The wattmeter reading at rated current is approximately the full-load copper loss.
- Applying rated voltage would cause a dangerously high current. Only about 5% to 15% of rated voltage is normally needed.
- Because the applied voltage and flux are low, core loss is neglected.
