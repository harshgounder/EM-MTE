# Induction Motor: Starting, Running, and Slip Example

## Starting condition

\[
N_s=\frac{120f}{P}
\]

At start, rotor speed is zero, so:

\[
s=1
\]

Rotor frequency equals supply frequency at standstill:

\[
f_2=f
\]

The rotor impedance at standstill is:

\[
Z_2=\sqrt{R_2^2+X_2^2}
\]

The corresponding standstill rotor-current magnitude is:

\[
I_2=\frac{E_2}{\sqrt{R_2^2+X_2^2}}
\]

## Running condition

Rotor induced frequency is:

\[
f_2=\frac{(N_s-N_r)P}{120}=sf
\]

At slip \(s\), the rotor impedance is:

\[
Z_2=\sqrt{R_2^2+(sX_2)^2}
\]

## Worked problem

A 3-phase induction motor has 2 poles and is connected to a 400 V, 50 Hz supply. Calculate synchronous speed, rotor speed, and rotor frequency when slip is 4%.

\[
N_s=\frac{120(50)}2=3000\,\text{rpm}
\]

\[
N_r=(1-s)N_s=(1-0.04)(3000)=2880\,\text{rpm}
\]

\[
f_2=sf=0.04(50)=2\,\text{Hz}
\]

In normal running condition, core loss is relatively small because the rotor frequency is low.
