# Transformer Rating, Short-Circuit Parameters, and Regulation

## Why transformers are rated in kVA

Core loss mainly depends on applied voltage, and copper loss mainly depends on current:

\[
P_{cu}=I^2R
\]

Neither loss directly depends on load power factor. Therefore a transformer is rated by apparent power, \(\text{kVA}\), rather than real power, \(\text{kW}\).

## Values from the short-circuit test

At rated current during the short-circuit test:

\[
Z_{eq}=\frac{V_{sc}}{I_{sc}}
\]

\[
R_{eq}=\frac{W_{sc}}{I_{sc}^2}
\]

\[
X_{eq}=\sqrt{Z_{eq}^2-R_{eq}^2}
\]

Because core loss is negligible in this test, \(W_{sc}\) is approximately the full-load copper loss.

Rated voltage and rated current are the full-load voltage and current of the respective winding.

## Voltage regulation

Voltage regulation is the change in secondary terminal voltage from no load to full load, at a specified power factor:

\[
\%\text{VR}=\frac{V_{20}-V_2}{V_2}\times100
\]

For negligible no-load drop, \(V_{20}\approx E_2\), so:

\[
\%\text{VR}\approx\frac{E_2-V_2}{V_2}\times100
\]

where \(V_{20}\) is the no-load secondary voltage and \(V_2\) is the loaded secondary terminal voltage.

The approximate voltage drop is:

\[
E_2-V_2\approx I_2R_{eq}\cos\phi_2 \pm I_2X_{eq}\sin\phi_2
\]

- Use \(+\) for lagging power factor.
- Use \(-\) for leading power factor.
