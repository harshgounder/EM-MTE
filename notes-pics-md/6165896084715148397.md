# Transformer Loads, Losses, and Voltage Regulation

## Loss dependence

- Copper loss depends on load current, so it varies with load:

  \[
  P_{cu}=I^2R
  \]

- Core loss depends mainly on applied voltage and frequency. At fixed supply voltage and frequency, it is approximately constant.

## Load types and power factor

| Load type | Typical example | Current relative to voltage | Power factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resistive \(R\) | Heater, geyser | In phase | Unity |
| Inductive \(L\) | Induction motor | Lags | Lagging |
| Capacitive \(C\) | Capacitor bank | Leads | Leading |

Unless a question specifies a resistive or capacitive load, a transformer load is often treated as inductive, so the power factor is lagging.

## Practical transformer on load

For a lagging-power-factor load, the approximate secondary voltage drop is:

\[
E_2-V_2\approx I_2R_{eq}\cos\phi_2+I_2X_{eq}\sin\phi_2
\]

For a leading-power-factor load, replace the plus sign with a minus sign.

## Transformer losses

1. Core, or iron, loss:
   - Hysteresis loss.
   - Eddy-current loss.
2. Copper loss.

Both hysteresis and eddy-current loss depend on frequency and flux density.

## Voltage regulation

One common convention is:

\[
\%\text{VR}=\frac{E_2-V_2}{E_2}\times100
=\frac{I_2R_{eq}\cos\phi_2\pm I_2X_{eq}\sin\phi_2}{E_2}\times100
\]

Some texts use \(V_2\), rather than \(E_2\), in the denominator. State the convention if the question specifies one.

### Zero regulation at a leading power factor

For zero voltage regulation with a leading load:

\[
I_2R_{eq}\cos\phi_2-I_2X_{eq}\sin\phi_2=0
\]

\[
\tan\phi_2=\frac{R_{eq}}{X_{eq}}
\]

\[
\phi_2=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{R_{eq}}{X_{eq}}\right)
\]

Thus voltage regulation is zero at a leading power factor with this angle.
