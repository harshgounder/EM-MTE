# Electrical Machines

## Motors and generators

- A **motor** converts electrical energy into mechanical energy. It is identified from its input.
- A **generator** converts mechanical energy into electrical energy. It is identified from its output.

## Electromagnetic induction

- \(\Phi\) is magnetic flux.
- \(N\) is the number of turns in a coil.
- Induced emf is proportional to the number of turns:

  \[
  e \propto N
  \]

- **Faraday's law**:

  \[
  e = -N\frac{d\Phi}{dt}
  \]

  The negative sign expresses Lenz's law: the induced emf opposes the change that produces it.

- A fixed magnetic field can use DC excitation. To obtain a changing flux, use AC excitation.
- With a static magnetic field, emf can still be generated by relative motion between the conductor and the magnetic field. This is called **dynamically induced emf**.

> Check: the source says that induced emf cannot be DC. More precisely, the polarity and magnitude of an induced emf depend on the rate of change of flux linkage. A steady relative motion commonly gives an alternating emf, but a unidirectional changing flux can produce a unidirectional emf.

## Transformer

A transformer is a static electromagnetic device that transfers electrical energy from one circuit to another without changing the frequency.

- Its efficiency is generally high, commonly above 95%.
- It consists of copper windings and an iron core.
- Its operating principle is electromagnetic induction.

### Step-up transformer

A step-up transformer increases voltage by increasing the number of turns on the secondary winding:

\[
N_2 > N_1, \qquad V_2 > V_1, \qquad I_2 < I_1
\]

For an ideal transformer:

\[
\frac{V_1}{V_2}=\frac{N_1}{N_2}=\frac{I_2}{I_1}
\]

The source's final ratio was written as \(V_1/V_2 = I_2/I_1\); the turns ratio is included above because it is part of the ideal-transformer relation.
