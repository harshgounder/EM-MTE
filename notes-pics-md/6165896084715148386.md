# Transformer Equations

## Induced-emf equation

For sinusoidal core flux, the rms induced emfs are:

\[
E_1 = 4.44fN_1\Phi_m
\]

\[
E_2 = 4.44fN_2\Phi_m
\]

Since \(\Phi_m=B_mA\), the primary emf equation can also be written as:

\[
E_1=4.44fN_1B_mA
\]

where:

- \(f\) is the supply frequency in hertz.
- \(N_1\) and \(N_2\) are the primary and secondary turns.
- \(\Phi_m\) is the maximum (peak) core flux in weber.

For an ideal transformer, terminal voltages equal the corresponding induced emfs:

\[
E_1=V_1, \qquad E_2=V_2
\]

## Voltage and turns ratio

\[
\frac{V_2}{V_1}=\frac{E_2}{E_1}=\frac{N_2}{N_1}=k
\]

Here \(N_1/N_2\) is commonly called the primary-to-secondary turns ratio, while \(k=N_2/N_1\) is the transformation ratio used in these notes.

## Impedance transformation

With impedances referred to corresponding sides of an ideal transformer:

\[
\frac{Z_1}{Z_2}
= \frac{V_1/I_1}{V_2/I_2}
= \frac{V_1I_2}{V_2I_1}
= \left(\frac{N_1}{N_2}\right)^2
\]

Thus, impedance transforms as the square of the turns ratio.

## Maximum flux density

\[
B_m=\frac{\Phi_m}{A}
\]

where \(B_m\) is the maximum flux density in tesla and \(A\) is the core cross-sectional area in square metres.
