# Transformer Efficiency, Continued and Maximum-Efficiency Example

## Completion of the 500 kVA example

At half load, copper loss is one-quarter of full-load copper loss:

\[
P_{cu,HL}=\frac{5119.3}{4}\approx1280\,\text{W}
\]

Using a 0.8 power factor gives approximately \(97.8\%\) efficiency, consistent with the exact calculation on the preceding converted page.

## Problem

In a 25 kVA, 2000/200 V transformer, iron loss and full-load copper loss are 350 W and 400 W, respectively. Calculate:

1. Efficiency at full load and 0.8 lagging power factor.
2. Maximum efficiency and the corresponding load.

## Solution

Given:

\[
S_{rated}=25\,\text{kVA}, \qquad P_i=350\,\text{W}, \qquad P_{cu,FL}=400\,\text{W}
\]

### 1. Full load at 0.8 lagging power factor

\[
P_{out}=25\times0.8=20\,\text{kW}
\]

\[
\eta_{FL}=\frac{20000}{20000+350+400}\times100
=96.39\%
\]

### 2. Maximum efficiency and corresponding load

At maximum efficiency:

\[
P_{cu}=P_i=350\,\text{W}
\]

The load fraction is:

\[
x=\sqrt{\frac{P_i}{P_{cu,FL}}}
=\sqrt{\frac{350}{400}}
=0.9354
\]

Thus the corresponding load is:

\[
S=25(0.9354)=23.385\,\text{kVA}
\]

The power factor for maximum efficiency is not stated. Following the source note, take unity power factor:

\[
\eta_{max}=\frac{23385}{23385+2(350)}\times100
=97.09\%
\]

At another power factor, the load for maximum efficiency remains \(23.385\,\text{kVA}\), but the numerical efficiency changes.
