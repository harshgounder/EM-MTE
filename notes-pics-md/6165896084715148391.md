# Transformer EMF per Turn, Worked Examples

## Problem 1

The emf per turn of a 10 kVA, 2200/220 V, 50 Hz single-phase transformer is 10 V. Calculate:

1. Primary and secondary turns.
2. Net core cross-sectional area for a maximum flux density of \(1.8\,\text{T}\).

### Solution

Since emf per turn is \(10\,\text{V/turn}\):

\[
N_1=\frac{E_1}{10}=\frac{2200}{10}=220\ \text{turns}
\]

\[
N_2=\frac{E_2}{10}=\frac{220}{10}=22\ \text{turns}
\]

Using \(E_1=4.44fN_1B_mA_c\):

\[
2200=4.44(50)(220)(1.8)A_c
\]

\[
A_c=0.0250\ \text{m}^2
\]

> Check: the source writes \(0.03\,\text{m}^2\). Direct substitution gives \(0.0250\,\text{m}^2\), or \(250\,\text{cm}^2\).

## Problem 2, started on this page

A single-phase transformer has 380 primary turns and 1080 secondary turns. The net core cross-sectional area is written as \(550\,\text{cm}^2\). The primary winding is connected to a 400 V, 50 Hz single-phase supply. Calculate:

1. Maximum flux density in the core.
2. Voltage induced in the secondary winding.

### Calculation from the stated data

Convert the stated area:

\[
A_c=550\times10^{-4}=0.055\ \text{m}^2
\]

\[
B_m=\frac{400}{4.44(50)(380)(0.055)}=0.0860\ \text{T}
\]

The secondary voltage is:

\[
E_2=E_1\frac{N_2}{N_1}=400\frac{1080}{380}=1136.84\ \text{V}
\]

> Check: the handwritten intermediate line and its value \(0.026\,\text{T}\) are not consistent with the written data. The values above follow the data as legibly written; the next page may clarify the intended area or turns.
