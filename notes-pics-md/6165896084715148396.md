# Transformer Test Results, Equivalent Circuit, Part 2

This page completes the short-circuit calculation for the 50 kVA, 2400/120 V transformer in the preceding image.

## Short-circuit test, measured on the high-voltage side

Given:

\[
W_{sc}=810\,\text{W}, \qquad I_{sc}=20.8\,\text{A}, \qquad V_{sc}=92\,\text{V}
\]

```text
       R_eq       jX_eq
 V_sc ──/\/\/──────LLLL──────
   │                            │
   └────────────────────────────┘
```

Because the test is performed on the high-voltage side, the values first calculated are referred to that side:

\[
R_{eq,H} = \frac{W_{sc}}{I_{sc}^2}
=\frac{810}{20.8^2}
=1.872\,\Omega
\]

\[
Z_{eq,H}=\frac{V_{sc}}{I_{sc}}
=\frac{92}{20.8}
=4.423\,\Omega
\]

\[
X_{eq,H}=\sqrt{Z_{eq,H}^2-R_{eq,H}^2}
=4.007\,\Omega
\]

## Refer the series branch to the low-voltage side

The required side is the low-voltage winding. The turns ratio is:

\[
a=\frac{V_H}{V_L}=\frac{2400}{120}=20
\]

Referred impedances divide by \(a^2=400\):

\[
R_{eq,L}=\frac{1.872}{400}=0.00468\,\Omega
\]

\[
X_{eq,L}=\frac{4.007}{400}=0.01002\,\Omega
\]

\[
Z_{eq,L}=0.00468+j0.01002\,\Omega
\]

> Check: the source labels \(1.872\,\Omega\) and \(4\,\Omega\) as low-voltage-side values. They are actually high-voltage-side values, since the short-circuit test was conducted on the high-voltage side. The conversion above gives the requested low-voltage-side parameters.
